# Schwifty Product Vision

## Product purpose

Schwifty is a workforce scheduling and personnel-management application
designed for complex, shift-based operations.

It helps supervisors build schedules, manage staffing requirements,
coordinate overtime and mutual shift exchanges, monitor employee
competencies, and communicate an accurate working schedule.

## Primary users

### Supervisors and administrators

They need to:

- Build and modify schedules efficiently
- Identify staffing gaps and conflicts
- Manage overtime opportunities
- Review and approve mutual shift exchanges
- Track employee qualifications and competency coverage
- Publish reliable schedules
- Understand workforce trends

### Employees

They need to:

- Quickly understand when and where they are working
- View schedule changes
- Claim available overtime
- Request mutual shift exchanges
- Access the application effectively from a phone
- Trust that the displayed schedule is current and accurate

## Product promise

Schwifty should make complex workforce scheduling feel clear,
controlled, and dependable.

A user should be able to understand current staffing conditions and
complete common scheduling actions without unnecessary navigation or
training.

## Product principles

### Operational clarity

Important staffing information, conflicts, gaps, approvals, and schedule
changes must be immediately visible.

### Functional density

Schwifty handles substantial operational information. The interface
should improve scanability without hiding useful information or creating
excessive whitespace.

### One connected system

Schedule, overtime, mutuals, personnel, competencies, and metrics should
feel like connected parts of one workforce-management workflow.

### Trustworthy actions

Publishing, approving, deleting, merging, or overwriting information
must have clear consequences and appropriate confirmation.

### Preserve context

Users should retain date, team, schedule, filters, and page context while
moving through related workflows.

### Desktop power, mobile accessibility

Desktop interfaces should support dense scheduling and administration.
Mobile interfaces should emphasize viewing, responding, approving, and
completing focused actions.

### Progressive complexity

Common actions should be immediately accessible. Advanced configuration
should remain available without overwhelming routine workflows.

## Product character

Schwifty should feel:

- Professional
- Capable
- Calm
- Efficient
- Modern
- Purpose-built
- Trustworthy

It should not feel:

- Playful or gimmicky despite the Schwifty name
- Like a generic analytics dashboard
- Decorative at the expense of usability
- Sparse or inefficient
- Overloaded with disconnected controls

## Core product model

The main schedule is the authoritative published schedule.

Sub-schedules are focused planning contexts that can be reviewed and
published or merged into the main schedule. They should feel like
alternate schedule contexts rather than separate applications.

Overtime and mutual requests should connect clearly to the schedule and
affected employees.

Personnel and competency information should support scheduling decisions
rather than operate as isolated administrative records.

Metrics should help supervisors identify actionable workforce patterns,
not merely display decorative charts.

## Success criteria

The redesign is successful when users can:

- Understand schedule coverage and exceptions quickly
- Navigate between related workforce workflows naturally
- Complete frequent actions with fewer unnecessary steps
- Compare employees, shifts, qualifications, and requests efficiently
- Use the application at common desktop sizes without losing context
- Perform essential employee actions comfortably on mobile
- Trust the status and consequences of publishing or approval actions
